# Problemi, pain point e bisogni insoddisfatti delle micro e piccole imprese italiane (focus Nord: Lombardia, Piemonte), 2025-2026

Research date: 14 Oct 2026. Older data is marked with its year. Most figures come from trade associations (Confartigianato, Confcommercio, CNA, CGIA), which lobby for SMEs, so their framing may overstate burdens. Istat and Unioncamere figures are official.

## 1. Structure and digital intensity of Italian firms

### Takeaway
Italy has about 4.6 million active firms, and about 95% of them have fewer than 10 employees. Among firms with 10+ employees (Istat does not survey micro firms in "Imprese e ICT"), digital basics are now widespread: 68% use cloud and 56% use management software. AI adoption doubled to 16.4% in 2025, but the gap between SMEs and large firms on AI is widening (37 points). Lack of skills is the number-one barrier. The gap is presumably larger for micro firms, which are not measured.

### Cited Findings
**Structure**
- 2023: 4.617 million active firms with 18.644 million employees — [Istat Annuario 2025 cap. 14](https://www.istat.it/storage/ASI/2025/capitoli/C14.pdf)
- 2022: more than 4.2 million micro firms (up to 9 employees), 94.5% of the total, generating 27.2% of value added (Istat Rapporto annuale) — [Istat via search result](https://www.istat.it/storage/ASI/2025/capitoli/C14.pdf). Other Istat releases (year unclear) give micro firms 94.9% of firms, 47.8% of employees and 31.1% of value added — [Istat comunicato](https://www.istat.it/?p=18288)
- June 2025: 314,824 business owners aged 70+, 10.7% of sole proprietorships (Unioncamere-InfoCamere data) — [Wall Street Italia](https://www.wallstreetitalia.com/?p=804131)

**Digital intensity (Istat "Imprese e ICT 2025", Dec 2025, firms with 10+ employees)** — all from [Istat comunicato Imprese e ICT 2025](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/imprese-e-ict-anno-2025/)
- Almost 80% reach a basic level of digital intensity (at least 4 of 12 activities); 38.1% reach a high level (at least 7 of 12). Large firms: 96.4% and 81.4%.
- Progress toward the Digital Decade 2030 targets: SMEs at basic digital level 88.3% of target (68.1% in 2023); cloud 90.7% of target; data analysis 56.9%; AI only 21.9% of target.
- Cloud (intermediate/advanced): 68.1%. Management software: 56.0% (+7 points vs 2023). ERP: SMEs 48.8% vs large 85.9%. CRM: SMEs 21.1% vs large 56.5%.
- Online sales: 14.7% of firms; 19.1% in trade; 34.6% in accommodation and food services.
- Social media use: 59.0%; 72.8% in trade; 82.3% in accommodation and food services.
- Data analysis: SMEs 41.9% (25.7% in 2023) vs large firms 83.6%.
- AI: 16.4% of firms in 2025 (8.2% in 2024, 5.0% in 2023). SMEs 15.7% (7.7% in 2024) vs large 53.1%. The gap went from about 20 points (2023) to 25 (2024) to 37 (2025). Northwest Italy: 19.3% (8.9% in 2024). 83.6% use no AI.
- Uses of AI: marketing and sales 33.1%, administration 25.7%, R&D 20.0%. 33.4% of AI-using firms state no specific purpose, and 83.3% of those have 10-49 employees, which suggests experimental, unstructured use.
- Barriers among firms not using AI: lack of skills 58.6%, unclear legislation 47.3%, missing or poor data 45.2%, privacy concerns 43.2%, high costs 43.0%.
- European comparison for AI adoption: Germany 26%, Spain 20.2%, France 18.1%, Italy 16.4% — [Agenda Digitale / Istat](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/?p=252557)

**ICT spending**
- Politecnico di Milano (Osservatori Startup Thinking / Digital Transformation Academy) forecast ICT budgets for 2025 at +1.5% overall, +3.7% for small firms and +4% for medium firms. SME investment priorities: cybersecurity 31%, cloud 25%, Industry 4.0 24% — [EconomyUp](https://www.economyup.it/innovazione/trasformazione-digitale-in-italia-nel-2025-verso-una-crescita-degli-investimenti-ma-serviranno-piu-competenze/)
- Assintel Report 2025 puts the Italian ICT market at €44.3bn (+4.5%). Spending grows more slowly at micro (+1.7%) and small (+3.3%) firms — [Corriere Comunicazioni](https://www.corrierecomunicazioni.it/digital-economy/ict-mercato-a-quota-443-miliardi-assintel-lancia-lagenda-digitale-per-pmi-e-pa/); [Confcommercio Milano CS Assintel](https://www.confcommerciomilano.it/doc/news_comunicati/pdf/2025/Cs-Assintel-Report-2025.pdf)
- Lombardia 2025 (Excelsior): among firms that invested in digital transformation, the most common areas were cybersecurity (42%) and high-speed internet, cloud, mobile and big data (40%) — [Unioncamere Lombardia, Excelsior bollettino annuale 2025](https://www.unioncamerelombardia.it/fileadmin/dati__file_report_trimestrali/Mercato_del_lavoro/Previsioni_fabbisogni_occupazionali/Bollettini_annuali/2025/LOM_Boll_anno2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Micro firms (under 10 employees, 95% of all firms) are outside Istat's ICT survey. Their digitalisation is almost certainly lower than the 10-49 bracket. This is a large market that official data does not measure.
- AI is the steepest gap: small firms use AI without a clear purpose and cite lack of skills (59%) and data quality (45%) as barriers. That points to demand for done-for-you AI setup, training and vertical AI tools rather than horizontal platforms.
- CRM adoption among SMEs (21%) is low compared with ERP (49%). Customer management, follow-up and marketing automation are under-served.

### Gaps
- No Istat or DESI data on digital intensity of firms with 0-9 employees. No Lombardia- or Piemonte-specific ICT data in the Istat release.
- No reliable data found on average annual software spend per micro firm.

## 2. Top documented pain points

### Takeaway
The best-quantified pain points are four. Credit to small firms shrank by about 26% from 2019 to March 2026, and by 39% for artisans. 47-48% of planned hires are hard to fill, and over 65% for skilled trades in Lombardia. Bureaucracy costs €57bn a year, about 238 hours per small-business owner. Electricity costs are about 22% above the EU average. Neighbourhood shops are also closing fastest in the North, cyberattacks on Italian firms rose 42% in 2025, and succession planning is missing at most small firms.

### Cited Findings
**Bureaucracy and compliance**
- CGIA di Mestre (using The European House Ambrosetti data) estimates bureaucracy costs Italian firms about €57.2bn a year, with a range of €57-80bn depending on method. In 2024 firms had to follow 305 Gazzette Ufficiali plus 45 supplements, 35,140 pages — [Money.it, May 2026](https://www.money.it/la-burocrazia-costa-alle-imprese-italiane-piu-delle-tasse-i-numeri-nascosti)
- Confartigianato: a small-business owner spends an average of 238 hours a year on bureaucratic tasks, 56 more than the OECD average — [Money.it](https://www.money.it/la-burocrazia-costa-alle-imprese-italiane-piu-delle-tasse-i-numeri-nascosti)
- Older Confartigianato estimate (year not shown): €16.6bn a year, an average of €12,334 per firm, with 76.3% of the cost falling on micro firms under 10 employees — [Blitz Quotidiano archive](https://archivio.blitzquotidiano.it/economia/imprese-burocrazia-sprechi-sud-nord-510531); a related figure of €16.8bn sits within a €60bn total "zavorra" of tax, credit, energy and labour costs — [L'Adige](https://www.ladige.it/attualita/confartigianato-da-fisco-a-caro-tassi-zavorra-da-60-miliardi-s7fly1lc)

**Access to credit**
- From 2019 to March 2026, loans to small firms fell by €34bn (-25.9%) and to artisans by 39.3%. Total business lending fell 7.3% over the same period. In March 2026 small-firm lending was down 4.3% year on year (-4.0% at end 2025) and artisan lending down 8.8%. Interest rates for SMEs range from 6.5% (Bolzano) to 10.6% (Sardegna), against a 4.97% average (Confartigianato, Aug 2026) — [Teleborsa/Borsa Italiana](https://www.borsaitaliana.it/borsa/notizie/teleborsa/economia/pmi-credito-piu-scarso-e-costoso-in-sette-anni-finanziamenti-giu-del-259-37_2026-08-10_TLB.html?lang=it)
- Libro bianco sul credito alle MPI (2025): in H2 2025 total business credit grew 1.2%, while credit to micro and small firms fell about €5bn, close to €70bn lost over 15 years. Stated causes include information asymmetry, the high cost of assessing small loans, weak collateral enforcement and the closure of local bank branches — [Il Giorno / Unioncamere](https://www.ilgiorno.it/economia/ultimaora/unioncamere-credito-a-micro-e-7420ac5a)
- Local example: Modena, 2025, small-firm loans -3.1% vs +5.6% for all firms (Banca d'Italia data via CCIAA) — [CCIAA Modena](https://www.mo.camcom.it/informazione-economica/informazione-economica/news/allegati/banca-ditalia-molto-positivo-landamento-dei-risparmi-e-dei-prestiti-nel-2025)

**Late payments**
- Cerved Osservatorio Pagamenti (2025 data, Apr 2026): average payment times fell, to 50.2 days for micro firms, 56.9 small, 63.2 medium and 73.2 large. Delays beyond agreed terms, however, increased in 2025 for micro, small and large firms — [Teleborsa](https://www.teleborsa.it/DettaglioNews/51_2026-04-13_TLB/Cerved-calano-i-tempi-di-pagamento-delle-imprese.html); [Quotidiano.net](https://www.quotidiano.net/economia/finanza/pagamenti-alle-aziende-sempre-piu-63b2a59c)
- Q3 2025: average delays rose for micro firms from 8.6 to 9.7 days and for small firms from 6.8 to 7.5 days (Cerved Payline, 3M+ firms) — [Milano Finanza](https://www.milanofinanza.it/news/pagamenti-delle-imprese-italiane-si-interrompe-il-miglioramento-nel-terzo-trimestre-2025-ritardi-in-crescita-202601021101282411)
- September 2025: 43.6% of payments on time and 4.3% more than 90 days late. This comes from a TeamSystem article; the primary source is not identified — [TeamSystem](https://www.teamsystem.com/magazine/fintech/crediti-deteriorati/)

**Labour shortage (Excelsior, 2025)**
- Italy 2025: 5.8 million planned hires. Firms reported difficulty filling 47% of them, mainly because of a lack of candidates (30%) and, less often, inadequate preparation (13%). Difficulty rate by education: university degree 52%, secondary school 47%, compulsory schooling 44% — [Unioncamere Lombardia, Excelsior bollettino 2025 (national intro)](https://www.unioncamerelombardia.it/fileadmin/dati__file_report_trimestrali/Mercato_del_lavoro/Previsioni_fabbisogni_occupazionali/Bollettini_annuali/2025/LOM_Boll_anno2025.pdf)
- Lombardia 2025: 1,033,310 planned hires (1,081,900 in 2024), 62% of employers hiring, 48% hard to fill (49% in 2024). 53% of hires are at firms with 1-49 employees — [same source](https://www.unioncamerelombardia.it/fileadmin/dati__file_report_trimestrali/Mercato_del_lavoro/Previsioni_fabbisogni_occupazionali/Bollettini_annuali/2025/LOM_Boll_anno2025.pdf)
- Lombardia, share hard to fill by occupational group: skilled workers (operai specializzati) 66.5%, technical roles 56.1%, machine operators 50.0%, high-skill/intellectual roles 47.6%, retail and service workers 44.5%. By business area, technical and design roles are 65.3% hard to fill.
- Lombardia, most-demanded professions: restaurant workers 127,890 hires (52% hard to fill), cleaning 85,600 (39%), sales staff 81,600 (25%), delivery and logistics 72,730 (33%), drivers 44,830 (56%).
- Lombardia, hardest roles for under-30 hires: construction finishing workers 80%, artisan mechanics and maintenance technicians 75%, metalworkers and toolmakers 73%, application developers 73%, engineering technicians 71% — [same source](https://www.unioncamerelombardia.it/fileadmin/dati__file_report_trimestrali/Mercato_del_lavoro/Previsioni_fabbisogni_occupazionali/Bollettini_annuali/2025/LOM_Boll_anno2025.pdf)
- Restaurants and hospitality: FIPE reported 2025 consumption of €96bn (+1.6%). A 2026 report cited by Gambero Rosso shows employee headcount falling by about 114,000 (-10.3%) to about 1 million in 2025, attributed partly to demographics and staff shortages. This conflicts with other FIPE statements that employment grew — [Gambero Rosso](https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/attualita/ristoranti-bar-in-calo-italia/); [Avvenire](https://www.avvenire.it/economia/lavoro/consumi-in-ripresa-nei-bar-e-ristoranti-stoppani-luoghi-di-coesione-sociale_92958). Older FIPE figures: 235,000 hires needed April-August 2023, with nearly 40% hard to find, led by waiters (about 55,000) and cooks (about 30,000) — [Avvenire 2023](https://www.avvenire.it/economia/lavoro/ristorazione-235mila-addetti-da-assumere-entro-agosto_66358)

**Generational succession and ageing owners**
- CNA survey of 2,000+ entrepreneurs: nearly 1 million SMEs risk closing because of difficulty passing the business on, and more than half have taken no concrete steps. Family handovers succeed in 63.7% of cases. Sales to employees or third parties fail mainly for lack of buyers and of financing — [La Rassegna](https://www.larassegna.it/il-passaggio-generazionale-su-misura-della-micro-e-piccola-impresa/?print=pdf)
- Only about 2 in 10 children of entrepreneurs want to continue the family business. Cases where the business cannot stay in the family rose 60% in ten years — [Avvenire](https://www.avvenire.it/economia/il-passaggio-generazionale-mette-in-crisi-le-aziende-funziona-meglio-in-famiglia_107695)
- Bergamo: 28.22% of 23,652 commerce, tourism and services firms have an owner or manager aged 60+ (Ascom Confcommercio Bergamo) — [Wall Street Italia](https://www.wallstreetitalia.com/?p=804131)

**Closure of neighbourhood shops (desertificazione commerciale, Confcommercio 2025)**
- About 140,000 shops lost in 12 years, with about 105,000 vacant premises, roughly a quarter of them empty for over a year — [Avvenire](https://www.avvenire.it/economia/avanza-il-deserto-commerciale-in-12-anni-persi-140mila-negozi_100998); [L'Indipendente, Nov 2025](https://www.lindipendente.online/2025/11/16/in-italia-sono-stati-chiusi-140-000-negozi-negli-ultimi-12-anni/?pdf=165626)
- Fixed retail outlets fell from 666,479 (2018) to 570,313 (2025), -14.4%. The annual rate of decline reached 3.1% in 2025, up from 2.2% in earlier analyses. The national vacancy rate is about 15.1%. The North is hit hardest: Belluno, Vercelli, Trieste, Alessandria, Savona and Gorizia lost more than 33% of shops. Online purchases are 11.3% of spending on goods and 18.4% on services — [ItaliaOggi](https://www.italiaoggi.it/economia-e-politica/economia-e-finanza/desertificazione-commerciale-in-italia-i-dati-confcommercio-sulla-chiusura-dei-negozi-kfkb71vo); [Quotidiano.net](https://www.quotidiano.net/economia/chiusi-96mila-negozi-in-otto-44bcc4a7)
- Piemonte: more than 13,000 shops closed in 12 years. Meanwhile restaurants grew by 723 (+21.3%) and accommodation by 122 (+30.5%) in the 8 provincial capitals — [Il Piccolo](https://www.ilpiccolo.net/2025/04/14/piemonte-chiusi-oltre-13mila-negozi-in-12-anni-allarme-desertificazione-commerciale)
- Note: the Confcommercio reports use different periods and definitions (some include street vendors), so the figures are not directly comparable.

**Energy costs**
- Micro and small Italian firms pay about 28 c€/kWh, 22.5% above the EU average. Taxes and levies are more than double the EU average. Extra cost versus the EU: €1.6bn (Confartigianato, Oct 2025) — [LaPresse](https://www.lapresse.it/economia/2025/10/01/confartigianato-granelli-spread-dellenergia-pesa-su-pmi-16-mld-extra-costo-rispetto-a-ue/). A September 2026 Confartigianato update reportedly puts the extra cost at €3.7bn; the methodology is unverified — [Quotidiano.net](https://www.quotidiano.net/economia/pmi-caro-energia-analisi-confartigianato-0bcc6808)
- Energy prices are up about 50% since 2021 — [LaPresse](https://www.lapresse.it/economia/2025/10/01/energia-i-prezzi-sono-cresciuti-del-50-dal-2021/). CNA: Italian wholesale electricity prices were consistently the highest among major EU countries from 2019 to H1 2025 — [CNA Piemonte Nord](https://piemontenord.cna.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CS_11_23_06_2025_osservatorio_energia.pdf)

**Cybersecurity**
- Rapporto Clusit 2026 (2025 data): 507 known serious incidents in Italy (357 in 2024), +42%, a record; attacks on manufacturing +16% — [Pasquale Pillitteri summary](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/news/401/rapporto-clusit-2026-cybersecurity-italia); [01net](https://www.01net.it/rapporto-clusit-2026-cybersecurity-ai-ransomware/)
- Clusit Manufacturing 2026 (H1 2026): 45 incidents in Italian manufacturing, 18.4% of all Italian incidents (12.4% in 2025), 89% of them ransomware — [Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/news/19228/clusit-manufacturing-2026-attacchi-industria-italiana)
- Clusit's Security Summit 2026 presentation describes SME manufacturers and supply-chain subcontractors as the "unregulated Italy" (outside NIS2), where incident growth is "real and structural" — [Clusit Security Summit PDF](https://api.securitysummit.it/files/69c1418335195c00025a851e/2026_PlenariaRapporto_Clusit_17marzo_securitysummit-Milano2026_1920x1080.pdf)

### Inferences
- The worst problems are concentrated in the North. Shop closures are steepest in northern cities such as Vercelli and Alessandria in Piemonte. Lombardia's skilled-trade shortage reaches 66-80%. Lombardia also accounts for about 1 million planned hires a year.
- Lending to small firms is shrinking even as overall credit recovers. Banks find small loans too costly to assess, which leaves room for alternative credit scoring, invoice financing and other fintech for micro firms.
- Succession is a large, under-served transfer-of-business market: about 1 million SMEs at risk, more than half without a plan, and buyers lacking for transfers outside the family. Small-business brokerage or matchmaking platforms for artisans and shops could address it.
- Ransomware against small subcontractors outside NIS2 creates demand for managed security services and backups sized for micro firms.

### Gaps
- No national 2025 Excelsior breakdown found specifically for tourism; the Lombardia bulletin covers restaurant workers.
- No dedicated quantitative data found on the cost of HACCP, workplace-safety training (D.Lgs. 81/08) or GDPR compliance per micro firm, or on the time spent on e-invoicing and electronic receipts.
- No Confartigianato burden estimate specifically for 2025 was found; the €57bn figure comes from CGIA.
- No Piemonte-specific data on credit or succession was found.

## 3. New obligations that create demand (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Several obligations took effect in 2025-26 and give micro firms a deadline-driven need for help: mandatory catastrophe insurance, the POS-to-cash-register link, the RENTRI digital waste registry for small producers of hazardous waste, and the AI/privacy rules. Penalties range from losing access to public grants to fines of up to €4,000 and business suspension.

### Cited Findings
- **Mandatory catastrophe insurance (L. 213/2023, Legge di Bilancio 2024):** firms must insure buildings, plant, machinery and equipment against earthquakes, floods, landslides and inundations. Agricultural firms are excluded. Deadline for micro and small firms: 31 Dec 2025. Micro and small restaurants and hospitality firms got an extension to 31 Mar 2026 (DL Milleproroghe 200/2025); fishing and aquaculture to 31 Dec 2026. There is no direct fine, but non-compliance counts against the firm when public grants and subsidies are assigned — [Fiscal Focus](https://www.fiscal-focus.it/news-24/ore-08-40-polizza-catastrofale-scadenza-il-31-marzo-2026-per-turismo-e-ristorazione,3,182958); [TeamSystem](https://www.teamsystem.com/magazine/fintech/polizza-catastrofale-obbligatoria-cosa-cambia/); [Centro Fiscale](https://centrofiscale.com/polizza-catastrofale-obbligatoria-2026/)
- **POS linked to the telematic cash register (from 2026):** the link is made online in the Agenzia delle Entrate "Fatture e corrispettivi" area. Sources disagree on dates: some say from 1 Jan 2026, others give operational start on 5 Mar 2026 with a 20 Apr deadline for POS already in use. Periodic updates apply afterwards, for example by 31 Jul for devices activated or changed in May. Fines reach €4,000, with suspension of activity from 15 days to 2 months, and up to 6 months for repeat offenders — [Money.it](https://www.money.it/collegamento-obbligatorio-tra-pos-e-registratore-di-cassa-le-imprese-rischiano-sanzioni-fino-a-eur4000); [TeamSystem](https://www.teamsystem.com/magazine/horeca/collegamento-pos-rt-sanzioni/); [Money.it 31 luglio](https://www.money.it/pos-e-registratore-telematico-obbligo-entro-il-31-luglio); [Fiscomania](https://fiscomania.com/collegamento-pos-registratore-cassa/)
- **RENTRI (digital waste tracking, D.M. 59/2023):** the third and final group covers initial producers of hazardous waste with up to 10 employees, including artisans, small professionals and workshops. Registration ran from 15 Dec 2025 to 13 Feb 2026. From 13 Feb 2026 the digital waste transport form (xFIR) is mandatory for hazardous waste, and the digital loading/unloading register applies from the registration date — [PuntoSicuro](https://www.puntosicuro.it/gestione-rifiuti-C-122/rentri-dal-15-dicembre-via-al-terzo-ultimo-scaglione-AR-26011/); [TeamSystem](https://www.teamsystem.com/magazine/gestione-impresa-e-partita-iva/rentri-prime-iscrizioni/); [ANCE Cremona circ. 017/26](https://cremona.ance.it/2026/01/16/circ-017-26-rentri-dal-ministero-dellambiente-indicazioni-su-prossime-scadenze/?print=pdf)
- **AI regulation:** 47.3% of non-AI firms cite unclear legislation as a barrier, and 43.2% cite privacy — [Istat](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/imprese-e-ict-anno-2025/)

### Inferences
- Each obligation creates a short window of demand for a compliance service: a broker or comparator for catastrophe insurance for micro firms, setup and monitoring of the POS-to-register link, and a RENTRI/xFIR service for mechanics, hairdressers, dentists, builders and similar artisans. These are typically sold through or alongside commercialisti and trade-association service companies (CAF, Confartigianato/CNA service centres).
- A "compliance calendar and done-for-you" subscription aimed at micro firms (deadlines, filings, reminders across tax, safety, environment and insurance) fits the 238 hours a year spent on bureaucracy.

### Gaps
- No official MIMIT figures found on catastrophe-insurance take-up among micro firms, or on average premiums.
- No data on how many firms registered with RENTRI or on the compliance rate.
- The exact current POS-RT deadlines should be checked on the Agenzia delle Entrate site.

## 4. What tools and services Italian SMEs buy or lack; price sensitivity

### Takeaway
Italian SMEs mostly buy through two channels: their commercialista and the large Italian software houses, led by TeamSystem (2.5M+ clients). Spending at micro firms grows slowly (+1.7%). The gaps are CRM, marketing, data and AI use, and cybersecurity. Direct evidence of willingness to pay is weak. The best proxies are subsidies (vouchers covering 50%) and the purchases firms make to meet obligations.

### Cited Findings
- TeamSystem reports about 2.5 million direct clients and 40,000 accountant clients, revenue of about €851m in 2023, and €1bn in 2024. In 2024, 1 in 5 invoices was processed with the AI built into its software, saving users 50% of their time (company claim) — [TeamSystem presentation ESTE, Jun 2025](https://www.este.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Presentazione-TeamSystem-ESTE-23-06-2025_2.pdf); [Forbes Italia BrandVoice](https://forbes.it/2025/04/04/ai-cloud-e-soluzioni-intelligenti-teamsystem-accelera-la-digitalizzazione-delle-imprese)
- Adoption gaps among SMEs with 10+ employees: CRM 21.1%, ERP 48.8%, online sales 14.7%, AI 15.7% — [Istat](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/imprese-e-ict-anno-2025/)
- 2025 SME ICT investment priorities: cybersecurity (31%), cloud (25%), Industry 4.0 (24%) — [EconomyUp / Polimi](https://www.economyup.it/innovazione/trasformazione-digitale-in-italia-nel-2025-verso-una-crescita-degli-investimenti-ma-serviranno-piu-competenze/)
- ICT spending growth 2025: micro +1.7%, small +3.3%, against +4.5% for the whole market — [Corriere Comunicazioni / Assintel](https://www.corrierecomunicazioni.it/digital-economy/ict-mercato-a-quota-443-miliardi-assintel-lancia-lagenda-digitale-per-pmi-e-pa/)
- AI barriers: high costs are cited by 43.0% of non-adopters — [Istat](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/imprese-e-ict-anno-2025/)

### Inferences
- The commercialista is the main gatekeeper. Tools that also make the accountant's work easier, or are sold through accountants (white-label or partner channels), are likely to scale faster than direct-to-SME sales. This is the playbook of Fatture in Cloud and TeamSystem.
- Price sensitivity is high: micro-firm ICT spending is growing slowly and 43% cite cost as an AI barrier. Products should be priced low per month, or co-financed by vouchers that cover 50% with a minimum spend of €4,000.

### Gaps
- No reliable survey found on typical monthly software or consulting spend for micro firms (restaurants, hairdressers, artisans). Price points of vertical SaaS products would have to be gathered from vendor pricing pages, which were not researched here.
- No Osservatorio Innovazione Digitale nelle PMI (Polimi) 2025 data was found on micro-firm spending.

## 5. Incentives and subsidies that create demand for services

### Takeaway
Grants co-finance 50-75% of digital and startup spending, which creates demand for the services and consultancy needed to apply. Transizione 5.0, however, was under-used because it was too complex. That complexity, and the fact that funds run out first-come-first-served, is itself a pain point that grant-consulting services can address.

### Cited Findings
- **Voucher Doppia Transizione Lombardia 2026** (Unioncamere Lombardia and the Chambers of Commerce): grant covering 50% of eligible costs, up to €10,000, minimum investment €4,000. Eligible costs are technology, consultancy and training; consultancy plus training must be 30-70% of the total. Applications opened 28 Jul 2026 — [Rete Agevolazioni](https://www.reteagevolazioni.it/bando-voucher-doppia-transizione-lombardia/); [CCIAA Milano Monza Brianza Lodi](https://www.milomb.camcom.it/documents/d/guest/cs-bando-doppia-transizione); [CCIAA Sondrio](https://www.so.camcom.it/files/allegati/C.S.%20n.%2027%20del%208.07.2026%20Bando%20Doppia%20Transizione_0.pdf). The 2025 predecessor, "Voucher digitali Lombardia 2025", offered up to €10,000 — [Unioncamere Lombardia](https://unioncamerelombardia.it/fileadmin/bandi/2025/Bando_voucher_digitali_2025/Bando_Voucher_digitali_Lombardia_2025.pdf); [PartitaIVA.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/voucher-digitali-lombardia/)
- **Transizione 5.0:** MIMIT declared the funds exhausted on 7 Nov 2025, before the 31 Dec deadline, and later applications went onto a waiting list. Reported uptake varies: about €2.5bn out of €6.3bn according to CNI; Informazione Fiscale called it a flop, with only about 30% used and about €4.5bn unspent. About €3bn was reprogrammed (March 2025). Commentators blame complex procedures and the method for calculating energy savings — [Informazione Fiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/Transizione-5-0-scadenza-agevolazioni-imprese-risorse-GSE); [CNI rassegna](https://www.cni.it/images/Rassegna_stampa/2025/40_31.pdf); [Agenda Digitale](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/industry-4-0/transizione-5-0-verso-la-riprogrammazione-delle-risorse-serve-chiarezza/); [CNA-Confartigianato memoria](https://www.confartigianato.it/storage/2025/12/CNA_Confartigianato_Memoria_AS-1718_DL-Transizione-5.0.pdf)
- **Resto al Sud 2.0 / Autoimpiego Centro-Nord (Invitalia, applications from 15 Oct 2025)**, for people aged 18-35 who are unemployed, inactive or in the GOL programme. Centro-Nord: voucher up to €30,000 (€40,000 with the increase), 65% grant on investments up to €120,000 and 60% from €120,000 to €200,000. This comes from a single source; check it against Invitalia. Sud: voucher of €40,000-50,000, 75% or 70% grants. Applications are assessed first-come-first-served until funds run out — [PartitaIVA.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/?p=97748); [Edotto](https://edotto.com/articolo/autoimpiego-centro-nord-e-resto-al-sud-20-a-chi-e-quando-spettano-i-contributi); [Informazione Fiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/resto-sud-sportello-domanda-novita-incentivi)

### Inferences
- Grants that require 30-70% of spending on consultancy and training (Lombardia voucher) directly fund small digital consultancies and trainers. A one-person firm can offer "voucher-ready" packages (for example an AI or cybersecurity setup worth €4,000-8,000, half paid by the voucher).
- The Transizione 5.0 under-use (about 30-40% of funds used) shows a gap in grant navigation and paperwork, especially for micro firms without an internal finance function.
- Autoimpiego Centro-Nord funds new self-employed businesses in Lombardia and Piemonte, which is relevant to a one-person startup there.

### Gaps
- Smart&Start Italia 2025-26 terms were not researched. No Regione Piemonte digital grants were found in this pass.
- No data on how many Lombardia vouchers were awarded, or on the average amount per firm.

## 6. Italian startups and companies solving SME problems (examples)

### Takeaway
Italian vertical SaaS for hospitality and services can scale: Smartness raised €47M, the largest Italian vertical-SaaS round. The usual exit is acquisition by TeamSystem, which bought Fatture in Cloud and several AI startups in 2025. Restaurants are well served by startups. Tradesmen, artisans and the professionals who handle compliance (RENTRI, safety, insurance) appear less served.

### Cited Findings
- **Smartness (Trentino):** SaaS for hospitality. Its €47M Series B is described as Italy's largest vertical-SaaS round, aimed at scaling AI operations (round date not stated in the snippet) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/smartness-raises-e47m-to-scale-ai-operations)
- **Fatture in Cloud (Bergamo, founded 2014):** invoicing for micro firms and freelancers. TeamSystem bought 51% in 2015, when it had 40,000+ users — [EconomyUp](https://www.economyup.it/startup/fatture-in-cloud-entra-nel-gruppo-teamsystem/)
- **TeamSystem acquisitions in 2025:** Normo.ai (generative AI for professionals, Oct 2025), xtream (AI, Sep 2025), MultiFatture and MultiCAF from Multidialogo, Brainware and Millesimo (software for condominium administrators, Jun 2025), plus ClicData and Sellsy abroad — [TeamSystem comunicati](https://www.teamsystem.com/ts-media/comunicati/); [TeamSystem CS Multidialogo](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/1602_CS_TeamSystem_Multidialogo.pdf)
- **Qodeup (Brescia):** ordering and payment app for independent restaurants, €2.5M seed led by The Techshop (2022) — [Finance Community](https://financecommunity.it/?p=147655)
- **WaitHero:** software suite for restaurants, €1M seed, about 1,500 restaurants (date not confirmed) — [StartupItalia](https://startupitalia.eu/?p=485002)
- **Restworld (Torino):** job-matching platform for restaurants, €1M seed (Dec 2024), which addresses the restaurant staff shortage — [Forbes Italia](https://forbes.it/2024/12/10/restworld-startup-finanziamento-ristorazione/)
- **Uala:** booking software for hairdressers and beauty salons, backed by Immobiliare.it with €5M (several years ago) — [StartupItalia](https://startupitalia.eu/startup/linnovazione-da-sempre-nel-nostro-dna-dal-sios-ai-round-multimilionari-la-storia-di-uala/)

### Inferences
- TeamSystem is an active buyer of AI tools for accountants and professionals, and of vertical SaaS for niches such as condominium administrators. That makes "AI for commercialisti, consulenti del lavoro and amministratori" a realistic route to an exit.
- Restaurants and hospitality are crowded with startups. Artisans, tradespeople and compliance services (RENTRI, workplace safety, catastrophe insurance, succession) appear less served by startups. This rests on the absence of funded examples in the search results, not on a systematic market map.

### Gaps
- No funding rounds from 2025-2026 were found specifically for SaaS for artisans or tradesmen, or for marketplaces for artigiani. A dedicated search on StartupItalia, EconomyUp or Dealroom is needed.
- Revenue and customer numbers for most of the startups listed are unverified.
