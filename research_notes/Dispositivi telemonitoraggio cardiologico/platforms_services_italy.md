Research notes. ## 1. Italian public infrastructure: PNRR national platform (PNT) and regional telemedicine infrastructures (IRT) — who built them, what they do, status in 2026

### Takeaway
Italy's public telemonitoring runs on a two-tier architecture funded by PNRR M6C1: one national infrastructure (INT/PNT, built and run under a concession by PNT Italia S.r.l. = Engineering 60% + Almaviva 40%) and 21 regional infrastructures (IRT), most of them bought through a single Lombardy/ARIA framework agreement (ARIA_2023_807, 2 lots, ~€279M, multi-supplier, awarded 2024 to several RTIs including ones led by GPI and Engineering). Devices/kits are procured separately by regions. As of early 2026 the PNT was operational and collecting data, but consolidated public data on telemonitoring uptake were lacking and post-June-2026 funding must come from ordinary SSN budgets. A private cardiologist cannot plug into the IRT directly; it is the channel for ASL/hospital (and accredited-provider) services.

### Inferences
- The IRT tender is a multi-supplier framework ("accordo quadro"): each region contracts specific RTI(s) under its lot, so the actual platform vendor differs by region (GPI-, Engineering-led RTIs and others such as Alfahealth). A cardiologist working for/with an ASL will use whatever IRT module the region chose; vendor choice is not his.
- Software (IRT) and devices (kits) are procured separately; this pushes regions towards device-agnostic integration via the AGENAS medical-system guidelines (see section 4) rather than closed vendor ecosystems.
- With PNRR funding ending mid-2026 and uptake data unconsolidated, the public channel in late 2026 is real but uneven by region; telemonitoring (vs. televisit) appears to be the least mature service.

### Gaps
- Exact list of RTIs/members awarded in each ARIA lot and which region contracted which RTI was not found in a primary source (ARIA esito/GUUE notice not read).
- No verified 2026 national figures on telemonitoring patients specifically (vs. all telemedicine).
- No source found on Almaviva's role in regional IRTs, nor on whether Dedalus or Exprivia are IRT awardees (Dedalus appears in Consip CCE tenders and Piedmont/Emilia-Romagna projects, not confirmed as ARIA awardee).

## 2. Italian private/market providers: platforms, telecardiology/telemonitoring service centres, home-care companies — offers, clients, certifications, costs

### Takeaway
For a private cardiologist or clinic in Italy the mature market is "service + platform" telecardiology: companies such as Cardio On Line Europe (Cardioline WebApp, 24/7 ECG reading centre), HTN – Health Telematic Network (Brescia, 24h service centre, multi-parameter telemonitoring), Telbios (Milan, class IIa platform, home kit with ECG/BP/scale/SpO2/temperature) and newer entrants (D-Heart, Cardio3) supply devices, a cloud platform and cardiologist telereporting, mostly to pharmacies, GPs, clinics and some ASLs. IT integrators (GPI, Engineering, Dedalus, Exprivia, Reply/Ticuro) serve hospitals/regions. Home-care gas companies (Vivisol, MedicAir, Air Liquide/Vitalaire, Linde) focus on respiratory telemonitoring; no evidence found of cardiology-specific kits from them in Italy. Public price lists are essentially absent.

### Inferences
- The typical private-market model in Italy is B2B2C: provider supplies (rents or sells) devices plus class IIa web platform plus cardiologist reading centre; the referring cardiologist/clinic/pharmacy pays per report or by contract; Cardio On Line Europe explicitly allows "in-house" reading by the client's own cardiologist, which suits a cardiologist who wants the platform but reads himself.
- Italian providers' strength is ECG/Holter/ABPM telereporting; continuous multi-parameter home telemonitoring with nurse triage is offered (Telbios, HTN) but with limited hours (Telbios 08–20) and older public documentation.
- €48/patient/year (hospital incremental cost) suggests software+staff costs for a public provider are low relative to device costs and reimbursement; private retail pricing will be much higher and must be quoted.

## 4. Device-agnostic vs closed ecosystems; interoperability (HL7 FHIR, IHE, IEEE 11073/Continua), gateways, integration with EHR/FSE; regulatory status (MDR) and data protection

### Takeaway
Two architectures dominate: closed device+dashboard ecosystems (Withings, Corsano, Philips ePatch, iRhythm, AliveCor, BioIntelliSense) and device-agnostic platforms/gateways (Tenovi, Validic, Huma, Luscii, Medixine, Italian IRTs). Italy's AGENAS 2025 "Linee di indirizzo" for telemonitoring medical systems explicitly push device-agnostic integration: IRTs must accept data automatically, via caregiver, or from pre-existing telemedicine systems, using HL7 FHIR, IHE, DICOM, REST APIs and FSE 2.0 vocabularies, with ISO 27001/27017/27018 security. Under EU MDR Rule 11, RPM software that monitors physiological processes is at least class IIa, and class IIb when monitoring vital parameters where variations could cause immediate danger; Italian platforms found (Cardioline WebApp, TelbiosConnect, Ticuro) are class IIa, Huma is IIb.

### Cited Findings
**AGENAS guidelines (telemonitoring medical systems)**
- AGENAS published "Linee di indirizzo" to classify the maturity of "Sistemi Medicali" (technology, functionality, connectivity, interoperability, security & data protection), to guide health-authority staff in choosing devices and to guide manufacturers' product evolution; date given in the text as 11 April 2025 (article metadata shows 20 Nov 2025 — discrepancy) — [Quotidiano Sanità](https://www.quotidianosanita.it/studi-e-analisi/telemonitoraggio-agenas-pubblica-le-linee-guida-per-l-evoluzione-dei-sistemi-medicali/)
- "Sistema Medicale" redefined as integrated hardware, firmware/software, communication networks and interoperability components, including physiological sensors (e.g. glucometers, ECG), clinical algorithms, mobile apps and APIs; standards named: HL7 FHIR, IHE, DICOM, RESTful APIs, FSE 2.0 Business Glossary; IRTs must receive data in three modes: automatic, caregiver-intermediated, or via pre-existing telemedicine systems; security: ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018, security-by-design, encryption, incident management — [Quotidiano Sanità](https://www.quotidianosanita.it/studi-e-analisi/telemonitoraggio-agenas-pubblica-le-linee-guida-per-l-evoluzione-dei-sistemi-medicali/)
- National telemedicine guidelines: DM Salute 21 Sept 2022 (GU 2 Nov 2022) sets functional requirements and service levels for telemedicine services — [Certifico](https://www.certifico.com/news/decreto-21-settembre-2022); requirements discussed include device-functioning monitoring and patient/caregiver training, and compliance with CEI EN 60601-1-11 (home-use electromedical equipment) — [AIIC presentation 2024](https://aiic.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/5-Giuliana-Faiella.pdf) (search summary)

**Device-level standards and gateways**

### Inferences
- When choosing a platform for use with Italian public structures, a cardiologist/clinic should require: FHIR/IHE interfaces (for IRT and FSE 2.0 upload of reports), MDR class IIa or higher certificate (IIb if real-time vital-sign alarms are intended), ISO 27001 (ideally 27017/27018 for cloud) and EU hosting; ACN qualification matters if selling to ASLs/hospitals.
- Several Italian platforms were certified under the old MDD; their MDR transition status should be checked (MDR transitional deadlines).
- Closed ecosystems simplify logistics (one vendor, cellular devices) but conflict with the AGENAS device-agnostic direction; device-agnostic platforms need either standard BLE profiles/IEEE 11073 or vendor-specific integrations via a hub.

## 5. Practical deployment: what a set-up looks like for a private cardiologist vs a hospital/ASL in Italy (rental kits, pay-per-patient, 24/7 monitoring centres)

### Takeaway
A private cardiologist in Italy realistically has three routes: (1) subscribe to an Italian telecardiology service (Cardio On Line Europe, HTN, Telbios, D-Heart, Cardio3) that rents/sells devices and provides a class IIa platform plus optional 24/7 cardiologist reading — he can read in-house or outsource; (2) license an EU CE-marked device ecosystem/SaMD platform (Withings, Corsano, Kardia, Luscii/Huma-type) and run nurse/physician triage himself, accepting no IRT/FSE integration; (3) partner with an ASL/hospital or accredited structure using the regional IRT (the only route into SSN-funded telemonitoring). Hospitals/ASLs use the regional IRT (ARIA framework), regionally tendered device kits, a regional/hospital monitoring centre (e.g., Puglia CoReHealth model), staffing around 1 nurse per 1,000 patients.

- Providers offer device rental or manual data entry options for home BP telemonitoring (HTN) and devices for sale or rental (Cardio3) — [HTN](https://www.e-htn.it/en/servizi-htn/professionisti-sanitari/); [ItaliaOggi/Cardio3](https://www.italiaoggi.it/native-content/cardio3-dove-il-battito-incontra-la-connessione-lih4prh4) (search summary)

- Pharmacies ("Farmacia dei Servizi") are a major channel for Italian telecardiology (ECG, Holter, ABPM) — [Cardio On Line Europe](https://www.cardioonlineeurope.com/Company-Profile-Cardio-On-Line-Europe.pdf); [D-Heart pharmacy brochure](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0653/3290/4160/files/DHeart_ECG_soluzione_telecardiologia_24_7_per_Farmacia.pdf)

- In Germany, Withings markets a cellular CHF kit built around national reimbursement rules — an example of reimbursement shaping kit design — [DMEA 2026](https://www.dmea.de/en/news-blog/exhibition-highlight-withings-health-solutions-at-home-heart-failure-monitoring.html)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable private set-up (inference): a class IIa (or IIb if alarms on vital signs) platform with clinician dashboard and thresholds; a kit of Bluetooth/cellular devices (ECG patch or handheld ECG, BP, SpO2, scale, thermometer) rented per patient; a documented triage protocol with defined service hours and explicit "not an emergency service" disclaimer (as Telbios does); GDPR DPIA, data-processing agreement with the vendor, EU hosting; informed consent; optional outsourcing of ECG reading to a 24/7 centre.
- For SSN-reimbursed telemonitoring, the cardiologist must operate within an ASL/hospital or accredited structure using the regional IRT; private standalone telemonitoring is self-pay (tariffs covered by another researcher).
- Given lack of public pricing, quotes from 2–3 Italian providers (Cardio On Line Europe, HTN, Telbios or Cardio3) plus one EU ecosystem vendor (e.g., Withings) are needed to size cost per patient/month.

### Gaps
- No real-world price points (per ECG report, per patient/month, kit rental) found for Italy.
- No documented case of a private Italian cardiologist practice running multi-sensor home telemonitoring (as opposed to ECG/Holter telereporting).
- Liability/medico-legal rules for alarms outside service hours not researched here.
