# Remote / continuous blood pressure monitoring (cuffless and connected-cuff), medically cleared SpO2 wearables and medical-grade smartwatches for cardiology telemonitoring (state as of November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: this is based on about 40 web searches run on 26 Nov 2026. The session's network blocked direct page fetches for several primary hosts (academic.oup.com, esc365.escardio.org, raps.org, medaval.ie, aap.com.au), so a number of figures below come from search-result summaries of primary documents (FDA 510(k) summaries, journal abstracts, manufacturer pages), not from full-text reads. Where this matters it is flagged. Dates are given so older information can be spotted.

## Q1. Cuffless and wearable BP products: technology, calibration, validation, regulatory status (FDA / CE MDR), EU/Italy availability, price

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the "cuffless" market splits into three groups. (a) Calibrated PPG pulse-wave-analysis wearables: Hilo/Aktiia (CE 2021, FDA OTC 510(k) July 2025, US launch July 2026), Samsung Galaxy Watch (cuff calibration every 28 days; sold in the US since March 2026 as a *wellness* feature with no FDA clearance), Biobeat, and Corsano (FDA-cleared for spot-check NIBP only). (b) Watches with a real micro-cuff, i.e. wrist oscillometry: Huawei Watch D2 and the new D3 (CE MDR, ISO 81060-2 validated), and the older Omron HeartGuide. (c) Calibration-free or hospital continuous systems: LiveMetric (radial sensor array), Caretaker VitalStream (finger pulse decomposition). Apple's FDA-cleared feature (Sept 2025) is a hypertension *notification* that shows no BP number, and its sensitivity is low (about 41%). No cuffless product appears on STRIDE BP or the US Validated Device Listing.

**Apple Watch Hypertension Notifications: passive PPG, ML-based, no BP value shown**
- FDA 510(k) K250507, cleared 11 Sep 2025. ### Inferences
- For EU/Italy cardiology telemonitoring, only Huawei Watch D2/D3 offers a watch BP reading that is CE MDR certified, validated to ISO 81060-2 in peer-reviewed form, and needs no PPG calibration, because it is a real oscillometric cuff at the wrist. Wrist position and the measurement posture still matter, and these are consumer devices with no clinician platform found in this research.
- The calibrated PPG devices (Hilo, Samsung, Biobeat) estimate BP relative to a cuff-derived baseline. The independent evidence in Q5 suggests they track BP *changes* poorly. That matters most for telemonitoring, whose purpose is detecting change (titration, nocturnal dipping).
- US regulatory status is now two-tier. There are FDA-cleared cuffless devices (Hilo, Biobeat, LiveMetric, Corsano spot-check, Apple notification) and "wellness" BP features (Samsung US, WHOOP) that FDA tolerates only if they avoid diagnostic claims. Clinicians should not treat wellness-labelled BP data as clinical measurements.
- Apple's FDA-cleared feature is a screening notification. With about 41% sensitivity it cannot rule hypertension out, and it does not support telemonitoring of known hypertensive patients, who are explicitly excluded from its intended use.

- Samsung BP app CE status in Italy/EU: not re-verified in this session (it has been marketed in Europe for years, but the CE class/MDR status was not confirmed).
- Sotera ViSi Mobile (hospital continuous NIBP/SpO2) and Philips cuffless offerings were not researched. No 2025–2026 status found.
- CardieX "CONNEQT Band": no clearance or launch found. The status of Omron HeartGuide in EU/Italy (and whether it is still sold) is unconfirmed.
- Garmin, Withings and other watches have no BP feature beyond cuff accessories (see Q2). No evidence of new cuffless BP clearances for them was found.

- Exact text of the Biobeat, LiveMetric and Caretaker accuracy data, and their CE MDR (versus legacy MDD) certificate status.
- Clinician-facing platforms and API/integration for Huawei and Samsung BP data in the EU: not found.

## Q2. Connected-cuff BP for telemonitoring (Bluetooth/cellular) and 24 h ABPM with remote upload

### Takeaway
Validated upper-arm oscillometric cuffs with connectivity remain the clinical workhorse of BP telemonitoring. US RPM programmes rely on cellular cuffs (Tenovi, Smart Meter iBloodPressure, BodyTrace) that need no smartphone and appear on the AMA/AHA Validated Device Listing. Withings (BPM Pro 2 cellular, BPM Vision) and Garmin (Index BPM) sell FDA-cleared connected cuffs, and Omron runs RPM services (VitalSight US, Hypertension Plus UK). Information on cloud-connected 24 h ABPM devices and their Italian distribution was sparse.

- **Withings BPM Pro 2**: cellular upper-arm cuff with Wi-Fi/Bluetooth fallback, FDA cleared December 2024 and CE marked. Designed for care teams and RPM (for example, heart failure programmes). ECG is available only in Europe pending FDA clearance — [Mobihealthnews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/withings-gets-fda-clearance-bpm-pro-2-cellular-monitor); [Withings BPM Pro 2](https://www.withings.com/health-solutions/bpm-pro-2); [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/cardiology/20241224/fda-clears-novel-athome-bp-pulse-rate-monitor)
- **Withings BPM Vision**: FDA cleared, US launch 2 April 2025 at US$149.95, EU launch 6 October 2025. ECG expected in the EU Nov 2025 and the US in 2026 (per FAQ; status not re-verified) — [Withings US availability PR](https://media.withings.com/press/press-releases/bpm-vision-launch/BPM-Vision-US-Availability.pdf); [Withings FAQ](https://support.withings.com/hc/ja/articles/33546946659601-BPM-Vision-Frequently-Asked-Questions-FAQ); [Wareable CES 2025](https://www.wareable.com/ces/withings-unveils-new-smart-bpm-vision-blood-pressure-monitor)

## Q3. What cardiology/hypertension societies, standards bodies and FDA say about cuffless BP (ESH 2022/2023, ESC 2024 guidelines and 2026 statement, AHA/ACC 2025, AHA 2025 statement, ISO 81060-3, FDA 2026 draft guidance)

### Takeaway
Societies on both sides of the Atlantic agree: cuffless devices should **not** currently be used to diagnose, track or treat hypertension. Validated cuff devices remain the standard. ESH defined a dedicated six-test validation framework (2023). ISO 81060-3:2022 covers continuous devices. FDA issued its first draft guidance on cuffless clinical testing in January 2026. Regulatory clearance is explicitly said not to guarantee accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **ESH 2022 statement** (Working Group on BP Monitoring and Cardiovascular Variability, J Hypertens): cuffless devices have considerable potential, but their specific accuracy issues make cuff-device validation protocols inadequate. Cuffless BP devices "should not be used for the evaluation or management of hypertension in clinical practice" — [Ovid / J Hypertens 2022](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/jhypertension/fulltext/10.1097/hjh.0000000000003224~cuffless-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-review-and); [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/cuffless-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-review-and-statement-by/); quote via [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/fedprac/article/263558/hypertension/cuffless-blood-pressure-monitors-still-numbers-game/page/0/2)
- **ESH 2023 validation recommendations** (Stergiou et al., J Hypertens 2023;41(12):2074–2087, doi 10.1097/HJH.0000000000003483), for *intermittent* cuffless devices (readings at least 30 s apart). The six tests are: (1) static test (absolute accuracy); (2) device position test (hydrostatic effect); (3) treatment test (BP decrease); (4) awake/asleep test (BP change); (5) exercise test (BP increase); (6) recalibration test (calibration stability over time). Which tests apply depends on whether the device needs user calibration, measures automatically or manually, and is used in more than one position — [PubMed 37303198](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37303198/); [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/european-society-of-hypertension-recommendations-for-the-validati/)
- **ESC 2024 hypertension guidelines**: a secondary review states the ESC and ESH guidelines contain no formal recommendations on cuffless devices (not verified in the guideline text) — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12027560/); [ESC Essential Messages 2024 HTN](https://escardio.org/static-file/Escardio/Guidelines/Products/Essential%20Messages/2024%20EM/Essential%20Messages_2024%20HTN.pdf)
- **ESC scientific statement 2026** ("Cuffless BP Monitoring Devices: Technical Foundations and Clinical Implications"; ESC WG e-Cardiology, ESC Council on Hypertension, EAPC). Published in Eur J Prev Cardiol 2026;33(7):1058, doi 10.1093/eurjpc/zwag058. "Due to insufficient accuracy validation, this scientific statement does not recommend their use in clinical decisions." It names potential uses such as widespread out-of-office monitoring and detection of nocturnal hypertension — [Eur J Prev Cardiol (OUP)](https://academic.oup.com/eurjpc/article/33/7/1058/8497606); [Univ. Twente record](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/cuffless-blood-pressure-monitoring-devices-technical-foundations-/); [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/92047) (full text not accessible in this session)
- **2025 AHA/ACC High BP Guideline** (Aug/Sept 2025): "Reliance on cuffless devices, including smartwatches, for accurate BP measurements should be avoided until these devices demonstrate greater precision and reliability." It encourages home monitoring with validated cuffs and team-based care — [AHA Top Things to Know](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-high-blood-pressure-guideline\top-things-to-know); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/nephrology/20250903/top-10-prevention-takeaways-from-the-2025-high-blood-pressure-guideline); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/public-health/prevention-wellness/new-bp-guideline-5-things-physicians-should-know)
- **AHA Scientific Statement, Dec 2025** ("Cuffless Devices for the Measurement of BP", *Hypertension*, HYP.0000000000000254): cuffless devices should not currently be used to diagnose, track or treat hypertension, and validated cuffs should be used. Most cuffless devices need cuff calibration and some need demographic inputs, so accuracy depends on calibration quality, cuff accuracy and recalibration frequency. **FDA clearance does not require testing under a standardised accuracy protocol, so clearance does not guarantee accuracy.** Potential benefits are noted (sleep, activity, cuff-intolerant patients, under-resourced communities). Research priorities include standardised protocols, calibration stability and outcome data. Patients should tell clinicians where their readings came from — [AHA Professional](https://professional.heart.org/en/guidelines-statements/cuffless-devices-for-the-measurement-of-blood-pressure-a-scientific-statementhyp0000000000000254); [AHA Top Things](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/cuffless-devices-for-the-measurement-of-blood-pressure\top-things-to-know); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1109402); [AHA Hypertension](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYP.0000000000000254)
- The AHA statement also criticised Apple's hypertension feature: the clearance pathway required no validation against an international BP protocol, sensitivity is low, and performance across populations is unclear — [AHA Hypertension](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.125.26031); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/apple-watchs-hypertension-feature-may-miss-some-cases-researchers-find/812569/)
- **STRIDE BP / VDL**: STRIDE BP lists only cuff devices. AMA says several gaps must be addressed before cuffless devices could be added to the US VDL — [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/fedprac/article/263558/hypertension/cuffless-blood-pressure-monitors-still-numbers-game/page/0/2); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/public-health/prevention-wellness/don-t-trade-your-bp-device-cuffless-technology-just-yet); [Medaval STRIDE list](https://medaval.ie/docs/lists/BPMs-recommended-by-Stride-BP.pdf)
- **ISO 81060-3:2022** (Ed. 1, published Dec 2022): clinical investigation of *continuous* automated non-invasive sphygmomanometers, both trending and absolute-accuracy types, for any population or condition of use. It excludes intermittent devices (covered by ISO 81060-2) and does not assess motion artefacts. A device with an output period much longer than 30 s is not typically "continuous" — [ISO 81060-3](https://www.iso.org/standard/71161.html); [IEC webstore](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/33607)
- A late-2024 preprint states that no commercial cuffless device had completed the full ESH 2023 protocol or ISO 81060-3 (single source) — [medRxiv 2025.02.08](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.08.25321920v1.full.pdf)
- **FDA draft guidance** "Cuffless Non-invasive Blood Pressure Measuring Devices—Clinical Performance Testing and Evaluation": issued 23 Jan 2026, docket FDA-2025-D-0851, comments closed 24 Mar 2026, not final. Cuff devices (DXN) and NICU continuous NIBP (QYF) are out of scope. FDA generally recommends data on *static* accuracy (seated rest, per ISO 81060-2), *stability* and *response to BP changes*, tailored to technology and intended use. Novel intended uses may need De Novo or PMA. Cuffless devices are generally Class II — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/23/2026-01288/cuffless-non-invasive-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-clinical-performance-testing-and-evaluation); [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/cuffless-non-invasive-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-clinical-performance-testing-and-evaluation); [FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/190675/download); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/news-and-articles/news-articles/2026/1/fda-drafts-guidance-on-cuffless-blood-pressure-mea); [BioWorld](https://www.bioworld.com/articles/728084-fda-tackles-bp-wearables-in-new-draft-guidance); [Innolitics analysis](https://innolitics.com/articles/fda-cuffless-bp-guidance-analysis/)
- As of 2022, FDA had cleared 510(k)s for four cuffless devices (JAMA Cardiology, via RAPS) — [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-drafts-guidance-on-cuffless-blood-pressure-mea.html)

### Inferences
- In practice, cuffless BP data in a cardiology telemonitoring service should be used only for trend and context, with any clinical decision confirmed by validated cuff home BP monitoring or ABPM. This matches the ESH 2022, ESC 2026, AHA/ACC 2025 and AHA 2025 positions.
- A CE mark or FDA 510(k) on a cuffless device is not a proxy for meeting the ESH 2023 six-test framework. Procurement should ask vendors which ESH 2023 tests and which ISO 81060-2/-3 studies were passed, and whether they were independent.
- (Background knowledge, not re-verified this session.) ISO 81060-2 criterion 1 requires mean difference ≤ ±5 mmHg with SD ≤ 8 mmHg against reference, which is why cuffless studies often report "mean ± SD" pairs.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2025 ESH *update* statement on cuffless BP was found beyond the 2022 and 2023 documents. Whether ESH issued anything new in 2025–2026 is unconfirmed.
- Full text of the ESC 2026 statement and the exact acceptance thresholds of the ESH 2023 tests could not be read (host blocked / no full text in results).
- The ISO 81060-7 (intermittent cuffless) standard's status is unknown: no source returned.
- An Italian society position (SIIA, Società Italiana dell'Ipertensione Arteriosa) on cuffless devices was not searched.

## Q5. Independent validation studies and their results (Aktiia/Hilo, Biobeat, Samsung, Huawei, Corsano, Apple)

### Takeaway
Most favourable cuffless validations come from manufacturers or affiliated groups and use static, ISO 81060-2-style designs. The key independent study on Aktiia (Tan et al., J Hypertens 2023) found it failed to track nocturnal dipping and medication-induced BP falls: readings stayed near the calibration value. A 2025 negative Aktiia 24 h study was **retracted** in January 2026 over methodological problems (shared accounts), so it should not be cited either way. Samsung studies show accuracy near the calibration point that degrades as BP moves away from it. Huawei's cuff-based watch has the cleanest ISO 81060-2 evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Aktiia, developer-run 1-month study** (n=86, extended ISO 81060-2 adapted protocol, Blood Press Monit 2021): mean ± SD difference 0.46 ± 7.75 mmHg SBP and 0.39 ± 6.86 mmHg DBP, meeting ISO criteria 1 and 2 seated. - An earlier smartwatch-based BP study reported "insufficient accuracy" (PMC9309348; device per the article) — [PMC9309348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9309348/)

The earlier Huawei watch validation (2022) met both ISO criteria — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/specs/huawei/watch-d2/); [PubMed 36304535](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36304535/)
- **Biobeat**: FDA summary reports ISO 81060-2:2013-based validation. An independent assessor (Medaval) calls the protocol non-standard — [Innolitics K190792](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K190792); [Medaval](https://www.medaval.ie/resources/EN/devices/Biobeat-BB-613-Wrist-Watch.html)
- **Corsano 287-2**: cath-lab validation (n=97, against invasive) of 3.7 ± 4.4 / 2.5 ± 3.7 mmHg, from manufacturer reporting. Ambulatory validation is ongoing — [Corsano](https://corsano.com/validation-of-a-novel-cuffless-photoplethysmography-based-wristband-for-measuring-blood-pressure-according-to-the-regulatory-standard/)
- **LiveMetric**: n=34 against arterial line, 91%/92% of readings within 10 mmHg (company data) — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/livemetrics-blood-pressure-smartwatch-device-gets-fda-clearance/626445/)
- **Apple hypertension notifications**: the independent re-analysis is in Q1 (PPV about 69%, NPV about 79%) — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/apple-watchs-hypertension-feature-may-miss-some-cases-researchers-find/812569/)

### Inferences
- The recurring failure mode of calibrated PPG devices is "regression to calibration": good static agreement near the calibration BP, poor tracking of real changes (night dipping, drug titration). This is why ESH 2023 requires treatment, awake/asleep and recalibration tests, and why static ISO 81060-2 results alone are insufficient evidence for telemonitoring.
- The evidence base is small (n from 31 to about 100), often manufacturer-run, and sometimes methodologically fragile, as the 2026 retraction shows. Independent replication under the ESH 2023 or ISO 81060-3 frameworks is still missing for every commercial cuffless product found.

### Gaps
- No independent ESH-2023-protocol validation of Samsung, Hilo (current algorithm), Biobeat or Corsano was found.
- Full-text results (mean ± SD by condition) of Zhou et al. 2025 (Huawei D2) and of the Corsano EHJ-DH paper were not read directly.
- No independent validation of Huawei Watch D3 or of the Apple feature in European populations was found.
