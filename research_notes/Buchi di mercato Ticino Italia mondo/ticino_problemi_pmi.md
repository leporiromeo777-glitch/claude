# Problemi, pain point e bisogni insoddisfatti delle PMI in Canton Ticino (2025-2026)

Notes compiled 14 Oct 2026. Language: mixed English/Italian. Older data is flagged with its year. Where a figure came only from a search-result snippet and the article was not opened, it is marked "(snippet)".

## 1. Struttura dell'economia ticinese (aziende, dimensioni, settori, occupazione, frontalieri, salari)

### Takeaway
Ticino is an economy of micro-firms: about 40,600 establishments averaging 6.2 workers each (2022). It is 78% tertiary and depends heavily on cross-border labour: about 79-80k frontalieri, roughly 31-33% of jobs, and over 40% in retail and specialised construction. Wages run about 19-24% below the rest of Switzerland. Frontalieri numbers have plateaued since 2023, after the new tax agreement and the cantonal minimum wage.

### Cited Findings
**Business structure (STATENT 2022, USTAT Extra Dati, Nov 2024)**
- 2022: **40,610 establishments** (+18.5% vs 2011), **249,974 workers** (+17.3% vs 2011). Average size is **6.2 workers per firm** (tertiary 5.7; secondary 9.3, down from 10.1 in 2011; primary 3.0). USTAT concludes that "la dimensione media delle aziende ticinesi rimane comunque ridotta, confermando la prevalenza di piccole aziende". — [USTAT Extra Dati nov. 2024, ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- Sector shares of workers in 2022: tertiary **78.0%** (194,865), secondary **20.7%** (51,672), primary **1.4%** (3,437). Share of firms: tertiary 83.5%, secondary 13.6%, primary 2.8%. — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- Frontalieri share of workers: **30.7% in 2022** (24.6% in 2011). By sector: secondary 48.0%, tertiary 26.3%, primary 20.4%. Frontalieri numbered 76,739 in 2022, +46.3% vs 2011; in the tertiary sector they grew +79.6%. — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- **Retail (NOGA 47)**: 3,184 firms, about 15,000 workers (6.2% of jobs), flat since 2011. The frontalieri share in retail is **41.0%** (+12.5 percentage points since 2011). — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- **Construction**: about 20,000 workers in both 2011 and 2022. Main construction (NOGA 41-42) fell to 7,951 (-15.1%), with an average size of 8.3. Specialised trades (NOGA 43: electricians, plumbers, heating, finishing) rose to **12,439 (+14.4%)**, which is 5% of all jobs, with an average firm size of **5.1** and **43.0% frontalieri**. USTAT links the shift to **energy renovation work** ("risanamento energetico"). — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- **Health and social care**: health services grew +47.0% and residential/social care (NOGA 87-88) +45.1% over 2011-2022. They make up **8.2% and 5.4% of jobs**; about 74% of these jobs are part-time (by FTE). — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- **Finance and insurance** shrank: 13,038 to 12,074 workers (-7.4%), 4.8% of jobs, as branches closed and digitalisation advanced. — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)
- Older total (2021): 40,086 firms and 244,321 workers. — [USTAT prodima 4006 (Jan 2024)](https://m3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/prodima/4006_industria_e_servizi.pdf)
- **Manufacturing (Aiti/USTAT, June 2025, 2022 data)**: 1,969 manufacturing firms (peak 2,043 in 2014). **1,533 micro-firms with 1-9 employees (>77%)**, 314 with 10-49, 104 with 50-249, 18 with 250+. FTE 26,178 (27,246 in 2012). — [laRegione, 26.6.2025](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1849057/servizi)

**Labour market Q3 2025 (USTAT Notiziario 2025-42, 24 Nov 2025)**
- Employed persons (domestic concept) **242,169** (-2,831 y/y, about -6,000 vs the 2023 peak). Resident employed 169,331 (+1,853). **Frontalieri 79,812** (+26 y/y, flat), down from >81,000 in 2023. Over 5 years frontalieri grew +10.7% and residents +6.3%. — [USTAT Notiziario 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- USTAT attributes the trend reversal to "il nuovo accordo sulla tassazione dei frontalieri, l'introduzione di un salario minimo cantonale ma anche la congiuntura economica". — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- ILO unemployment **6.0%** (10,813 people), down from 7.3% a year earlier. Registered unemployed: 4,294. Activity rate 57.8%. **Recipients of AI/AVS pensions are almost 92,000** and rising. — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- Jobs: >254,000 (+0.9% y/y). Part-time is **36.1%** of jobs and rising (+3.6%); FTE about 206,000 (-0.1%). — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- Q4 2025: 78,809 frontalieri (-782 q/q, -143 y/y). In 2024 frontalieri were 33.0% of employed persons (annual average). Q2 2025: 79.4% of jobs were in services. — USTAT data as reported in search snippet (snippet), [search source tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/newsblog/valute-e-frontiere/1883100/ticino-frontalieri-grigioni-bfs-crescita-rallentamento)

**Wages**
- Private-sector median wage, 2022: **CHF 5,301 in Ticino vs CHF 6,510 in Switzerland**. A USTAT standardised table puts the rest of Switzerland at CHF 6,570, a **-23.9%** gap. The gap was 23.3% in 2020, so it is widening. Between 2008 and 2022 the Ticino median rose +7.5% against +12.5% nationally. — [USTAT ed_2025_03](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2025_03.pdf); [USTAT ed_2023-1](https://m3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2023-1.pdf) (snippet)
- 2024 (UST, all sectors): Swiss median **CHF 7,024**, Ticino **CHF 5,708**, about 19% lower. Ticino is "sempre fanalino di coda". — [RSI, salario mediano 2024](https://www.rsi.ch/info/svizzera/Nel-2024-in-Svizzera-il-salario-mediano-era-di-7%E2%80%99024-franchi-lordi--3305177.html); [RSI, Ticino fanalino di coda](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Salari-in-crescita-ma-il-Ticino-sempre-fanalino-di-coda--3306851.html) (snippet)

### Inferences
- About 40k firms averaging about 6 people means the addressable market is overwhelmingly micro-businesses without in-house IT, HR, marketing or finance staff. That suits low-touch SaaS and "done-for-you" services priced for 1-10-person firms.
- Lower wages and lower purchasing power cap what local SMEs will pay. Products priced at Zurich levels will struggle, so Ticino pricing probably has to sit between Italian and German-Swiss levels.
- Specialised construction trades (12.4k workers, about 5 workers per firm, growing on energy renovation) are a sizeable, fragmented, growing segment.

### Gaps
- No 2024/2025 cantonal GDP (PIL) figure was retrieved. The USTAT yearbook [tic_2025](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/tic_2025.pdf) probably has it.
- No exact count of all micro-firms (0-9) across all sectors in Ticino. STATENT shows that size class nationally is about 90%, but this was not verified for Ticino.

## 2. Problemi documentati delle PMI ticinesi

### Takeaway
The recurring pain points are: low local purchasing power plus cross-border shopping and the strong franc (retail); shortage of skilled and apprentice labour in trades, construction, health and hospitality, even though overall recruiting difficulty is below the Swiss average thanks to Italy; generational succession (a quarter to a third of firms); tourism seasonality; rising bankruptcies in 2025; and export firms hit by tariffs. Town-centre vacancy is reportedly low, but merchants describe retail as "resisting" rather than healthy.

### Cited Findings
**Labour and skills**
- Only **16.8%** of Ticino firms report difficulty finding qualified staff, against **36.3%** nationally (Q3 2025). USTAT credits integration with the Italian labour pool, which also "aumenta la competizione sul mercato del lavoro" and holds down wages. Vacant posts: about 1,300 (0.5% of jobs). — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- In 2024 USTAT noted that firms still struggle to find suitable workers, "particolarmente sentita nei settori industriale e delle costruzioni". — [USTAT news 2024-30](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/244075ns_2024-30.pdf) (snippet)
- **Apprenticeships**: in August 2025 **over 400 apprenticeship places were still vacant** in Ticino, about 1,500 contracts were signed, and the hardest-hit sectors were **construction, crafts, health, administration and electronics**. Some trades are unattractive to families because of the association with building sites. — [RSI, 2.8.2025](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Tirocini-in-Ticino-oltre-400-posti-ancora-vacanti--3015249.html)
- Aiti (June 2025) is concerned about a shortage of qualified labour and a growing shortage of medium- and low-skilled workers. A 2022 SUPSI study for Aiti foresaw a labour deficit accumulating over the next 10-15 years. — [laRegione 26.6.2025](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1849057/servizi)
- **Hospitality**: hotels and restaurants keep struggling to recruit. HotellerieSuisse Ticino admitted (2024): "Spesso ci si lamenta della carenza di personale, ma siamo noi i primi a non formarlo". GastroTicino listed the staff shortage as a central theme in 2023. — [laRegione temi ristorazione](https://www.laregione.ch/temi/ristorazione); [RSI, Ristoranti e hotel a corto di personale (2022)](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Ristoranti-e-hotel-a-corto-di-personale--1797538.html) (snippet)

**Generational succession**
- A 2013 cantonal event cited studies estimating that **1 in 4 Ticino firms** would face a change of ownership or management within 5 years (2013). — [DFE invito 2013](https://www4.ti.ch/fileadmin/DFE/DFE-EVENTI/2013/2013_invito_evento_Successioneaziendale_DEF.pdf) (snippet)
- A 2022 Gruppo Multi survey: **34% of Ticino firms** need a succession solution within 5 years (2022). — [We Wealth](https://www.we-wealth.com/?p=18045) (snippet)
- Nationally, D&B estimates that **more than 1 in 6 Swiss SMEs** is looking for a buyer, and the number will rise as baby boomers retire. Planning should start 3-5 years ahead, ideally 10. — [KMU portal SECO](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/it/una-gestione-attenta-delle-successioni-rafforza-leconomia-svizzera-in-modo-duraturo) (snippet)
- SUPSI notes that many successions will be outside the family (to competitors, MBO or MBI), and retiring owners increasingly co-finance (vendor loans). — [SUPSI DEASS seminar 2024](https://www.supsi.ch/documents/d/deass/240903_deass_cctg_aspetti-fiscali-della-successione-aziendale) (snippet)

**Retail, strong franc, cross-border shopping**
- Lugano merchants' association (Dec 2024): Ticino residents have "un potere d'acquisto nettamente inferiore" to the rest of Switzerland. "La spesa oltre confine" is a decisive competitive factor, and framework conditions "non sono ottimali". Federcommercio Ticino (Sommaruga) names the **strong franc, proximity to Italy and e-commerce**. Few empty windows mean only that retail "is holding on", not that it is doing well. — [tio.ch, 18.12.2024](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1802518/sono-poche-le-vetrine-vuote-pero)
- National vacant retail units: 2.4% fell to 2.1% in six months (2024 study). St. Gallen 8.6%, Basel 3.7%, Zurich 1.2%; no Lugano figure. — [tio.ch 18.12.2024](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1802518/sono-poche-le-vetrine-vuote-pero). SwissInfo gives a different figure: **3.4%** in 2024, back to pre-pandemic level, with about 1 in 5 non-food purchases made online. — [SwissInfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/ita/il-meglio-dei-contenuti-ssr/le-vetrine-vuote-una-minaccia-per-lattrattiva-delle-citt%C3%A0-svizzere/91151596). *The two studies use different methods.*
- Federcommercio describes a retail crisis "che si protrae da anni". — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1733258/poveri-ticinesi-spesa-in-italia-da-non-frenare-ed-e-polemica-come-uno-schiaffo) (snippet)
- USTAT retail survey, H1 2025: generally negative assessments, with a brief improvement in May reversed in June. — [USTAT news 2025-35](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/255380ns_2025-35.pdf) (snippet)

**Manufacturing and exports**
- USTAT/KOF manufacturing survey (Sept 2025): conditions are weaker because of uncertainty and **US tariffs** slowing exports; export-oriented firms are particularly negative. — [USTAT ed_2025_12 / news](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2025_12.pdf) (snippet)

**Bankruptcies**
- Jan-Sept 2025: 6,274 bankruptcy proceedings in Switzerland (+40% y/y) and **+34% in Ticino**. Partly an effect of the debt-enforcement law reform in force from 1.1.2025, which now sends public-law debts (e.g. AVS/tax) through bankruptcy. — [Dun & Bradstreet, Sept 2025](https://www.dnb.com/it-ch/notizie/fallimenti-e-fondazione-a-settembre-2025.html)
- 251 company bankruptcies in Ticino in the first nine months (year not shown in snippet). — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1705842/ben-251-fallimenti-aziendali-negli-ultimi-nove-mesi-in-ticino) (snippet)

**Tourism seasonality**
- Locarnese/Valli: **6.7 million overnight stays in 2025** (+2.6%). Strategy: "destagionalizzazione, grandi eventi, sport e outdoor". — [laRegione Locarnese](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/locarnese/1909035/servizi) (snippet; URL attribution not 100% verified)
- Winter 2025/26: Ticino hotel overnight stays **+7.2%** (Graubünden flat). — [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/info/svizzera/Il-Ticino-ha-riempito-gli-alberghi-lo-scorso-inverno--3804218.html) (snippet)
- In January 2025 several Luganese hotels were reported to be closing temporarily because low-season demand does not cover costs. Operators say de-seasonalisation needs shops, attractions and lifts as well as hotels. In December 2025 HotellerieSuisse Ticino (Sonja Frey) said December overnight stays were +35% over 2015-2025, but cantonal support for staying open off-season is hard to get. — reported in search snippets from [laRegione](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/bellinzonese/1875562/servizi) / [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/s/4136807) (snippet; exact article per claim not verified)
- Easter 2025: bad weather caused cancellations, hitting lower-star hotels and the valleys hardest; occupancy was about 60%. — [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Il-maltempo-rovina-anche-i-%E2%80%9Cconti%E2%80%9D-del-turismo--2767170.html) (snippet)
- IRE-USI O-Tur, autumn 2025: foreign arrivals +2.5% and overnight stays +2.6%; domestic arrivals +1.8% and stays +0.9%. — [IRE-USI O-Tur autunno 2025](https://www.ire.usi.ch/storage/app/media/rapporto%20O-Tur%20autunno%202025.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Labour scarcity in Ticino is **sectoral, not general**: trades, construction, health and care, hospitality, apprentices. Tools for recruiting, training or matching in Italian, and staffing for seasonal hospitality and trades, address a real pain point.
- Succession affects about a quarter to a third of firms, and most are micro-firms too small for M&A boutiques. An Italian-language marketplace or advisory service for small business transfers (valuation, matching, financing with vendor loans) is a plausible niche.
- The retail pain comes mostly from **demand** (purchasing power, cross-border shopping, e-commerce), not from vacancies. Opportunities lie in digital or omnichannel tools for local shops, loyalty and local-currency schemes, and services that draw Italian and tourist customers.
- The 2025 bankruptcy spike, partly regulatory, suggests demand for cash-flow and receivables management and for early-warning tools for micro-firms. This is an inference; no Ticino survey found on this.

### Gaps
- No 2025 Cc-Ti member survey results were found; the 2023 edition is the latest retrieved, see section 3. No quantitative data on Ticino town-centre shop vacancy (Lugano, Locarno, Bellinzona, Mendrisio).
- No 2024-2026 hard data on shortages of plumbers, electricians or other artisans beyond apprenticeship vacancies.
- No 2024-2026 Ticino-specific succession survey (e.g. D&B Ticino breakdown) was found.

## 3. Cosa dicono le indagini (Cc-Ti, Aiti, USTAT, IRE-USI, SUPSI, studi svizzeri digitalizzazione)

### Takeaway
Survey evidence specific to Ticino is thin and mostly older (Cc-Ti 2022-2023). No published Ticino SME digitalisation or AI survey results were found; SUPSI runs a 2025-2027 project on it. Swiss data shows AI adoption among SMEs rising quickly, from 22% to 34% between 2024 and 2025, with lagging data-protection practices.

### Cited Findings
- **Cc-Ti survey 2023** (305 member firms): good results despite energy prices, price increases and the strong franc for exporters; **77%** want legislation to regulate AI. — [laRegione (publiredazionale Cc-Ti)](https://www.laregione.ch/temablog/-publiredazionale/1721108/servizi) (snippet)
- **Cc-Ti survey 2022**: main concerns were energy costs and the availability and price of raw materials (director Luca Albertoni). — [laRegione](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1631672/servizi) (snippet)
- **Aiti / USTAT, June 2025**: document on firm number and size, staff characteristics and labour shortage, with figures listed in section 1. — [laRegione 26.6.2025](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1849057/servizi)
- **SUPSI** research project (2025-2027) on the impact of AI on Ticino SMEs, with a linked questionnaire on adoption of digital and cloud technologies in Ticino. No results published yet. — [SUPSI ARIS](https://aris.supsi.ch/items/a7d72569-fd54-40b3-8b73-bcbe655a61d7)
- **AXA SME study 2025** (300 SMEs, German- and French-speaking Switzerland, **Ticino not sampled**): **34%** of SMEs consciously use AI in operations in 2025, up from 22% in 2024; many lag on data protection. — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/svizzera/economia/1874160/ia-pmi-impiego-intelligenza-dati); [AXA blog](https://www.axa.ch/it/unternehmenskunden/blog/fondazione-e-innovazione/pmi-digitalizzazione.html) (snippet)
- Raiffeisen publishes an Italian edition of its SME study (KMU-Mittelstandstudie 2024). — [Raiffeisen PDF](https://raiffeisen.ch/content/dam/www/rch/firmenkunden/unternehmerthemen/mittelstandstudie/2024/kmu-mittelstandstudie-2024-it.pdf) (not read in detail)

### Inferences
- The AXA study excludes Ticino. Combined with the absence of a Ticino survey, this suggests Swiss digital/AI vendors and research largely skip the Italian-speaking region. That is consistent with an under-served localisation niche (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 results found for: Cc-Ti annual survey, BancaStato or UBS SME barometer for Ticino, ZHAW/FHNW KMU-Digitalisierung broken out for Ticino, GastroTicino member survey, Ticino Turismo staffing survey.
- Raiffeisen 2024 SME study content not extracted.

## 4. Invecchiamento della popolazione e bisogni conseguenti

### Takeaway
Ticino is one of the oldest cantons. Its old-age dependency ratio was 39.1% against 30.7% nationally (2020). The over-80s are projected to grow about 50% between 2018 and 2030, and nearly 92,000 people now receive AVS/AI pensions. Cantonal policy pushes "home first" care (day centres, 65+ help desks, support for family caregivers). This creates demand for home care, senior services and digital tools for families and caregivers, and adds pressure on business succession.

### Cited Findings
- Projections: over-80s **24,171 to 36,319 by 2030 (+50%)**; ages 65-79 from about 135k (2018) to nearly 174k. — [laRegione, pianificazione anziani](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1554245/popolazione-anziani-ticino-domicilio-pianificazione-assistenza-casa)
- Old-age dependency ratio **39.1% in Ticino vs 30.7% in Switzerland (2020)**. — [Pianificazione integrata LAnz-LACD 2021-2030, rapporto riassuntivo](https://www3.ti.ch/COMUNICAZIONI/191458/Rapporto_riassuntivo_Pianificazione_integrata_LAnz-LACD.pdf) (snippet)
- Need estimated for 2030: home care **1.64-2.17 million hours**; nursing-home beds **6,583-6,958**; extra public contributions of CHF 45.7-63.3 million. Measures: day centres, "sportelli 65+", support for **familiari curanti**, more training for care staff. — [Pianificazione LAnz-LACD](https://www3.ti.ch/COMUNICAZIONI/191458/Rapporto_riassuntivo_Pianificazione_integrata_LAnz-LACD.pdf) (snippet)
- Q3 2025: inactive people receiving AI/AVS pensions are **almost 92,000** and rising. — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- Residential and social care employment grew **+45.1%** over 2011-2022 (5.4% of jobs). — [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plausible small-business niches: coordinating private home help and care (alongside public SACD services), transport and companionship for seniors, home adaptation (a link to the growing specialised trades), digital literacy for seniors, Italian-language tools for family caregivers, and senior-friendly delivery and errand services in valleys with little public transport.
- An ageing owner base reinforces the succession problem in section 2.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 update of the LAnz-LACD planning was retrieved. No data on private (for-profit) home-care providers' market size in Ticino, or on waiting lists for nursing homes in 2025.

## 5. Dinamiche transfrontaliere (nuovo accordo frontalieri, consumatori, opportunità)

### Takeaway
The new Italy-Switzerland tax agreement took effect on 1.1.2024 for frontalieri hired after 17.7.2023. Combined with the cantonal minimum wage, it is associated with the plateau in frontalieri, which reverses a long growth trend. On the consumer side, Ticino residents shop heavily in Italy, which hurts local retail; Italy's lower VAT-refund threshold since 2024 makes Italian shopping more attractive.

### Cited Findings
- New agreement (signed 2020, replaces 1974 deal): applies to people who started working in Switzerland **after 17 July 2023**, effective **1 January 2024**. Switzerland withholds **80%** of the source tax; Italy then taxes with a credit. The Italian exemption is **€10,000** (raised from €7,500, Law 83/2023) and requires a daily return home (45 days/year tolerance). — [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/fisco-tasse/frontalieri-italia-svizzera-tasse-lavoro/941654/); [RSI, Frontalieri dal 2024 si cambia](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Frontalieri-dal-2024-si-cambia--1809908.html); [SUPSI fisco newsletter Jan 2024](https://sites.supsi.ch/fisco/dms/fisco/docs/pubblicazioni/articoli/NLLavoroGen2024-p03/NLLavoroGen2024%20p03.pdf) (snippet)
- The canton's stated aims for the agreement: more tax revenue and making cross-border wages less attractive. — [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Accordo-sui-frontalieri-si-riparte-da-un-disegno-di-legge--1805119.html) (snippet)
- An unverified estimate puts the extra burden on a "new" frontaliere earning CHF 80k at about €16k/year more than under the old regime. — [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/fisco-tasse/frontalieri-italia-svizzera-tasse-lavoro/941654/) (snippet; treat with caution)
- USTAT links the frontalieri slowdown to the new agreement, the cantonal minimum wage and the economy. — [USTAT 2025-42](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/news/256341ns_2025-42.pdf)
- Italy lowered the minimum purchase for a VAT refund for non-EU tourists to **€70** (from about €155) from **1.2.2024**. Bern considered halving the duty-free allowance from CHF 300 to 150; the Ticino government preferred the status quo, which DISTI (Ticino distributors' association) called a blow to local commerce. — [laRegione](https://www.laregione.ch/estero/confine/1722616/servizi); [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1733258/poveri-ticinesi-spesa-in-italia-da-non-frenare-ed-e-polemica-come-uno-schiaffo) (snippet)
- One estimate puts spending in Italy by Ticino border-area residents at **about CHF 500 million/year**, and a 2023 poll found **1 in 2 Ticino residents** shops in Italy to save money (sources not independently verified). — [InvestireOggi](https://www.investireoggi.it/spesa-estero-per-risparmiare-i-ticinesi-fanno-cosi/); [RSI Patti Chiari, La guerra dell'IVA](https://www.rsi.ch/la1/programmi/informazione/patti-chiari/inchieste/La-guerra-dell%E2%80%99IVA--2086239.html) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The new tax regime makes the paperwork for frontalieri and their employers more complex: dual returns, the 20 km zone rules, old versus new status, the 45-day tolerance. That suits niche payroll, tax-filing and HR-compliance services for frontalieri and the Ticino SMEs that hire them, alongside the fiduciarie.
- If cross-border hiring becomes less attractive, pressure grows on firms to find residents, automate and retain staff, which helps recruiting and automation tools.
- Cross-border shopping flows both ways. Ticino has the highest Swiss rental vacancy (section 7), so retail and office space is relatively accessible for new concepts.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 quantitative data on Italian consumers shopping in Ticino (e.g. fuel, tobacco, luxury, Swiss-made goods), or a fresh 2025 measure of Ticino residents' spending in Italy.
- Outcome of the federal duty-free allowance debate (CHF 300 to 150) not verified for 2025-2026.

## 6. Nicchia linguistica: servizi in italiano in Svizzera

### Takeaway
No source found directly measures the gap in Italian-language software and services for Swiss SMEs. Indirect evidence points to under-coverage: the main Swiss SME AI survey (AXA 2025) sampled only German- and French-speaking Switzerland, and Ticino-specific digitalisation data does not exist yet (SUPSI project 2025-2027).

### Cited Findings
- AXA SME/AI study 2025 sample: 300 firms in German- and French-speaking Switzerland only. — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/svizzera/economia/1874160/ia-pmi-impiego-intelligenza-dati) (snippet)
- SUPSI only began measuring digital and cloud adoption among Ticino SMEs in 2025. — [SUPSI ARIS](https://aris.supsi.ch/items/a7d72569-fd54-40b3-8b73-bcbe655a61d7)
- About 77% of Ticino manufacturers are micro-firms (1-9 employees), and the average firm across all sectors has 6.2 workers. — [laRegione 26.6.2025](https://www.laregione.ch/cantone/ticino/1849057/servizi); [USTAT ed_2024_15](https://www3.ti.ch/DFE/DR/USTAT/allegati/volume/ed_2024_15.pdf)

### Inferences
- Swiss compliance and business software (payroll, AVS/LPP declarations, VAT, cantonal forms, the 2025 debt-enforcement rules, frontalieri withholding tax) is typically built German-first. Italian software built for Italy does not handle Swiss law. A Ticino firm therefore needs **Swiss-law plus Italian-language** products, a small market (about 40k firms plus Italian-speaking Graubünden) that large vendors under-serve. This is an inference, not documented.
- Typical localisation targets: AI assistants and chatbots for Swiss-Italian customer service, templates and contracts under Swiss law in Italian, Italian-language training (AI, digital marketing, cybersecurity) for micro-firms, and frontalieri tax and payroll tools.

### Gaps
- No survey or article found that quantifies Ticino SMEs' dissatisfaction with German- or French-only software or services. This would need primary research (interviews with fiduciarie and Cc-Ti).

## 7. Notizie recenti (2024-2026) su gap specifici: artigiani, personale, hotel, negozi, casa, asili nido

### Takeaway
Recent news confirms vacant apprenticeships in trades and health (2025) and missing childcare places in some districts, especially Bellinzonese and Sottoceneri. Hotels close in the low season. Ticino is the exception to the Swiss housing shortage: it has the highest rental vacancy rate (2.08%) and asking rents fell in 2024-25. Bankruptcies rose in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Apprenticeships**: >400 places vacant in August 2025, mainly in construction, crafts, health, administration and electronics. — [RSI 2.8.2025](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Tirocini-in-Ticino-oltre-400-posti-ancora-vacanti--3015249.html)
- **Childcare**: places are missing in the Bellinzonese; the Council of State plans **+53 places by 2029** (laRegione, Oct 2025). — [laRegione](https://www.laregione.ch/1875384) (snippet). Nurseries rose from **55 to 74** since 2019, but children stay longer so fewer places free up for infants (RSI, May 2025). — [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Famiglia-e-lavoro-gli-sforzi-del-Cantone-per-la-conciliabilit%C3%A0--2813707.html) (snippet). A parliamentary question reports that each nursery on average turned away 15.5 children in one area vs 8.1 cantonal average, and estimates a need of **about 801 places** to reach 33% coverage, mostly in the Sottoceneri (date not verified). — [Gran Consiglio interrogazione 251.11](https://www4.ti.ch/fileadmin/POTERI/GC/allegati/interrogazioni/pdf/251.11.pdf) (snippet). Requests for "famiglie diurne" (home-based childminders) are rising. — [RSI](https://www.rsi.ch/info/ticino-grigioni-e-insubria/Famiglie-diurne-richieste-in-crescita--1980048.html) (snippet)
- **Housing**: Ticino has the **highest rental vacancy rate in Switzerland, 2.08%**. Asking rents fell **-5.6%** (Jul 2024-Jun 2025) while they rose +2.4% nationally (newhome / Swiss Real Estate Institute). — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/svizzera/cronaca/1868249/prezzi-spazio-citta-svizzera-capite) (snippet). The Swiss vacancy rate is about 1%, lower in big cities. — [Corriere del Ticino 19.11.2025 (PDF)](https://www.porsche-consulting.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/CdT%2019.11.2025.pdf) (snippet)
- **Hotels**: seasonal closures in the Luganese in January 2025; HotellerieSuisse Ticino says off-season support is hard to obtain (Dec 2025). See section 2 (snippet).
- **Bankruptcies**: +34% in Ticino, Jan-Sept 2025. — [D&B](https://www.dnb.com/it-ch/notizie/fallimenti-e-fondazione-a-settembre-2025.html)
- **Retail**: Federcommercio says there are few empty windows but the sector is "resisting", hit by the strong franc, Italy and e-commerce (Dec 2024). — [tio.ch](https://www.tio.ch/ticino/attualita/1802518/sono-poche-le-vetrine-vuote-pero)

### Inferences
- The childcare gap (hundreds of places, concentrated in Sottoceneri and Bellinzonese) is a concrete, locally documented unmet need. Small operators include nurseries, licensed home childminders, and holiday or after-school programmes. These are regulated businesses.
- Unlike the rest of Switzerland, Ticino has relatively cheap and available space, which lowers barriers for physical concepts and co-working.
- Low-season hotel closures point to a need for off-season demand generation (events, MICE, packages, remote-work stays), likely B2B services sold to the hotels themselves.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 article found that specifically documents a shortage of plumbers, electricians or other artisans, such as waiting times, beyond apprenticeship vacancies.
- No count of restaurant or hotel closures in 2025, and no GastroTicino 2025 data.
- No data on Lugano or Locarno town-centre shop closures in 2025-2026.
